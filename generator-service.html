Why Generac Recommends Annual Service

Generac specifies annual maintenance for all home standby generators, and most extended warranty plans require proof of regular service. Skipping service can:

  • Void your Generac warranty
  • Result in a failed start during the next outage
  • Allow carbon buildup to cause engine damage
  • Lead to stale fuel issues in the carburetor
  • Miss a developing battery or transfer switch problem

Warning Signs

Signs Your Generator Needs Service Now

Don't wait for a storm to discover your generator has a problem. Call us if you notice any of the following.

Won't Start or Auto-Transfer

If your generator doesn't start during a weekly self-test or fails to transfer power during an outage, this needs immediate attention.

Active Fault Codes / Alarm Light

Red warning lights or fault codes on the generator controller signal a real problem — often battery, low oil, or a sensor fault.

Unusual Smoke or Smell

Blue, white, or black smoke on startup — or a burning smell — indicate engine, oil, or electrical issues that need diagnosis.

Overdue for Annual Service

Generac recommends annual oil changes and inspections. If it's been more than 12 months since service, schedule it now before hurricane season.

Battery Warning

Generator batteries typically last 2–3 years. A low battery is the #1 reason generators fail to start when you need them.

Mobile Link Showing Offline

If your Generac Mobile Link app shows the generator as offline or in error status, there may be a communication or power issue to investigate.

What to Do If Your Generator Fails

1

Check the controller display

Note any fault codes or error messages shown on the display panel — this helps our technician diagnose faster.

2

Check breakers & fuel supply

Make sure the main breaker on the transfer switch is in the correct position and your fuel supply (gas or LP) is active.

3
